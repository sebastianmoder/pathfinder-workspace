# Adapting Learning Materials with AI

AI can draft a clearer explanation, a description of a diagram, captions, or a
translation. The educator must check that the adaptation preserves the meaning
and the learning students are expected to do. This guide extends the production
workflow in [Creating course materials](/teaching/course_materials).

CAST's Universal Design for Learning guidelines address multiple means of
engagement, representation, and action and expression, including language and
access to assistive technology.[^CAST] They provide a design framework, not
evidence that an AI-generated adaptation is accessible or educationally effective.

<Callout type="info">
  Use approved tools and permitted source material. Work from the barrier in the
  resource rather than requesting a student's diagnosis or personal history.
  Coordinate individual accommodations with the institution's accessibility team.
</Callout>

## Define what must stay the same

State the learning outcome, disciplinary terms, relationships, uncertainty,
numbers, and task demands that the adaptation must retain. Decide whether a
difficult feature is part of the outcome or an avoidable access barrier.
Shortening a problem can remove the reasoning it was intended to elicit.

| Adaptation | Useful draft request | Human check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clearer language | Shorter sentences with essential terms defined | Qualifiers, causal direction, numerical meaning, and disciplinary precision remain |
| Diagram description | Describe visible structure and relationships | The description matches the actual image and its educational purpose |
| Captions or transcript | Draft from a permitted recording | Technical terms, speakers, equations, and meaningful sounds are accurate |
| Translation or glossary | Preserve specified terminology and flag ambiguity | A competent reader checks meaning, register, notation, and language variety |
| Alternative format | Convert a resource to structured text | Headings, reading order, tables, links, and interaction work in the delivery format |

## Worked adaptation: preserve a statistical distinction

This short source is educator-authored for practice:

> In this observational sample, participants who attended the optional workshop
> had higher average scores. Because participation was self-selected, the
> comparison does not establish that attendance caused the difference.

**Flawed AI-style simplification:** “The workshop helped students get higher
scores.” It removes both the observational design and the limitation on a causal
conclusion.

**Reviewed adaptation:** “People who chose to attend the workshop had higher
average scores in this sample. They were not assigned to attend. The comparison
therefore does not show that the workshop caused the higher scores.”

Ask learners to identify the relationship and its limitation in both the source
and the adaptation. If interpreting observational evidence is the outcome, do
not supply the conclusion as an answer to an assessment question. This example
shows how to explain a distinction, not how to remove it from the task.

## Worked description: a chart without an invented trend

Suppose a teaching chart contains two labelled bars: Group A, 40%; Group B, 60%.
Its vertical axis runs from 0% to 100%. No group sizes, time points, or uncertainty
intervals are supplied. Provide the data table as an alternative representation.

**Flawed description:** “Performance improved by 20% after the intervention.”
The chart supplies neither a before/after comparison nor an intervention.

**Reviewed description:** “Bar chart showing 40% for Group A and 60% for Group B
on a 0–100% axis. B is 20 percentage points higher. Group sizes and uncertainty
are not given.”

Match the description to the activity. If the task assesses whether students
can calculate the difference, supply the values and structure without giving
the calculation away. Discuss an equivalent accessible representation with the
accessibility team. A fuller description can accompany an explanatory resource.

## Review captions and translations

Do not distribute a generated transcript without checking it against the
recording. For example, a missing “not” in “this result is not significant”
reverses the claim. Check spoken symbols, subscripts, and units against the
original visual or notes. Describe meaningful non-speech information and give
students a way to report errors.

For translation, maintain a short glossary of approved terms and ask the model
to flag ambiguous phrases rather than silently selecting a meaning. A second
AI translation back to the original language may expose issues, but is not
independent validation. If a competent reviewer is unavailable, use approved
language support or clearly limit the material's use; do not make an unverified
translation the sole route to essential information.

## Copyable adaptation prompt

```text
Draft an alternative version of this permitted teaching resource.
Audience and purpose: [context without personal student data]
Barrier to address: [language, visual access, format, or another barrier]
Learning outcome and challenge to preserve: [outcome]
Terms, numbers, qualifications, and relationships to retain: [list]
Source resource: [permitted material]

Return the adapted version and a comparison table locating each substantive
change in the original. Flag ambiguity, lost information, and anything that
requires specialist review. Do not add facts or certify accessibility. Do not
answer a question that students are meant to solve themselves.
```

## Verify the final resource

Review meaning against the source, then inspect the final document, learning
platform, slide deck, or media player. Text accuracy alone does not establish
correct reading order, keyboard access, caption timing, or screen-reader support.
Use institutional accessibility checks and involve relevant users without
requiring disclosure of personal circumstances.

Retain the editable original, a change record, and the reviewed adaptation.
Provide a correction route. Use the [pilot record](/teaching/evaluating_ai) to
check whether learners can access the material and still practise the intended
capability. For multilingual writing support and its assessment boundaries, see
[AI-assisted writing](/students/writing_instruction#targeted-language-feedback-for-multilingual-students).
