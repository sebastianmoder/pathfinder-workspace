# Facilitating a Teacher Workshop on AI

This two-hour pack helps educators review one teaching task and leave with a
testable redesign. It implements the [AI competency pathway](/students/ai_competencies)
through scenarios, source checking, and peer review. It is a proposed SAGE
workshop, not an accredited course or a demonstrated training intervention.

UNESCO's teacher framework includes a human-centred mindset, ethics, AI
foundations and applications, AI pedagogy, and professional learning, with
acquire, deepen, and create progression levels.[^UNESCO] The mapping below is
an interpretation for this workshop, not UNESCO endorsement or certification.

## Prepare the session

Invite participants to bring an assignment brief they are authorised to share,
without student work or confidential assessment items. Supply a fictional brief
from the [worked assessment designs](/teaching/assessment_examples) for anyone
who needs one. Ask them to name the learning students must still do themselves.

Prepare accessible copies of the scenario cards, worksheet, source-check
exercise, and answer key below. Provide these in advance in editable text, with
clear headings and readable tables. Arrange written as well as spoken
participation, a facilitator contact, and an appropriate break. No AI account is
required: all core activities use shared material and peer review.

If demonstrating a live tool, use an approved environment with synthetic inputs,
check it beforehand, and retain the static exercise as a backup. The facilitator
should know where to refer privacy, accessibility, security, and assessment
questions; they need not improvise institutional approvals.

## Two-hour schedule

| Time | Activity | Participant output | Competency emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 min | Welcome, purpose, and an individual first judgment | A learning outcome and initial view of AI's role | Human-centred mindset; acquire |
| 10–30 min | Compare the three scenario cards in pairs | A proceed, modify, escalate, or avoid decision with reasons | Ethics; acquire/deepen |
| 30–50 min | Inspect the source-check exercise | A corrected claim and a located source | Foundations and critical evaluation; acquire/deepen |
| 50–60 min | Break | — | Access and participation |
| 60–85 min | Redesign an assignment using the worksheet | One draft activity, AI rule, and learning check | AI pedagogy; deepen |
| 85–105 min | Peer review using the questions below | One consequential revision and its reason | Professional learning; deepen |
| 105–115 min | Plan a small evaluation | A comparison, success measure, and stop condition | Professional learning; preparation for create |
| 115–120 min | Individual exit task and next step | A justified decision on a fresh scenario | Transfer check; acquire/deepen |

The 120 minutes include instructions, transitions, and discussion. For a larger
group, use parallel pairs rather than adding a report from every group. Reduce
the scope of the redesign if participants need longer to read or deliberate.

## Scenario cards and facilitator notes

All scenarios are fictional. Provide them without the notes for the first
discussion, then compare judgments. Accept alternatives supported by the facts;
do not treat escalation as proof that a use is approved or prohibited.

| Card | Participant scenario | Facilitator notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | An educator wants three alternative discussion questions from their own public course summary. They will check each one before use. | A low-consequence candidate for proceeding after checking purpose and tool conditions. Ask what makes a question aligned and what review costs. |
| B | An educator plans to paste named student essays into an unapproved chatbot to generate feedback. | Stop this proposed workflow. Use synthetic work for practice or seek an approved environment and complete the responsible-use review before real work. Removing names alone may be insufficient. |
| C | A student team proposes using AI-generated customer interviews as evidence of demand for a campus service. | Modify the task: use the interviews only as labelled rehearsal, then investigate with approved real evidence or an educator-prepared pack. Link to entrepreneurial learning. |

Use [Responsible AI use](/genai/responsible_use) for governance and
[AI for entrepreneurial learning](/students/entrepreneurial_learning) for card C.
The point is to connect a decision with purpose, evidence, data, and consequence,
rather than classify AI as uniformly beneficial or harmful.

## Source-check exercise and answer key

Supply this fictional evidence card:

```text
S1: A voluntary survey received 40 responses. Thirty respondents said they
would like an evening seminar. The course enrols 200 students. No random
sampling or comparison with non-respondents was conducted.
```

**Intentionally flawed AI-style output:** “According to S1, 75% of the course
wants an evening seminar. This proves changing the timetable will improve
learning.” This is a prepared teaching artefact, not an observed model response.

Ask participants to label each claim as supported, unsupported, contradicted,
or unresolved, and write a justified correction. Give five minutes to work,
five to compare, and ten to discuss and review the key.

**Answer key:** 30 / 40 = 75% of respondents favour the seminar. S1 does not
establish the proportion of the entire course, because the responses are
self-selected. It contains no measure or causal comparison of learning. A
supported correction is: “Thirty of forty survey respondents favour an evening
seminar; wider demand and effects on learning remain unknown.” The citation is
real within this exercise but does not support the original claims.

Use the [claim-check record](/students/ai_literacy#make-evaluation-assessable) to
capture the judgment. Reward accurate interpretation, including retaining a
supported claim, rather than the number of errors found.

## Copyable redesign worksheet

```text
Original activity and learner context:
Learning outcome and thinking students must do themselves:
Proposed student actions and observable evidence:
AI role: AI-free / AI-limited / AI-permitted / AI-integrated
Allowed actions, prohibited actions, and proportionate disclosure:
Approved tool and permitted material, if a tool is needed:
Sources and educator verification:
Accessible participation and equivalent account-free route:
Independent or fresh-task learning check:
Expected preparation, review, and student workload:
Comparison with current teaching:
Success measure, unacceptable failure, and stop condition:
Decision owner and review point:
Peer-review revision and reason:
```

For peer review, ask: Which student action demonstrates the outcome? What could
AI accidentally do in the student's place? Which claim or output needs checking?
Can every student participate? Is the review burden realistic? What finding
would lead the educator to stop? The recipient should revise one substantive
decision or explain why it remains justified.

## Exit task and follow-up

Use a fresh scenario: “A tutor produces excellent solutions, but students cannot
solve a comparable problem without it.” Ask each participant to propose one
change and one learning check, explaining their connection to the outcome.
An appropriate response might reduce the help given, require an initial attempt,
and check a new independent problem. Satisfaction alone does not answer the
learning question. This short response samples judgment; it does not certify
competence across the framework.

Within two weeks, participants test their draft with synthetic examples or a
colleague, complete the [pilot record](/teaching/evaluating_ai), and report one
failure and resulting revision. Involving students requires the usual approvals
and access arrangements. Use the next meeting to inspect evidence rather than
only share impressive outputs.

Retain workshop worksheets only for an announced professional-learning purpose
in approved storage. Do not collect full chats or student records. Evaluate the
workshop through the quality of redesigned tasks and later application as well
as participant experience. Develop real case reports only when implementation,
permissions, results, and limitations are documented.
